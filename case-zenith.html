CASE 01 — WELLBEING

Zenith: plan, study, breathe, repeat

A self-management platform for university students, combining planning tools with study support and mental health resources — designed to help people stay consistent without feeling overwhelmed.

ROLEUX Research & Design
PLATFORMMobile (iOS)
TOOLSFigma, Lovable
FINAL BUILDLive prototype ↗

Overview

University life pulls students in a lot of directions at once — classes, deadlines, jobs, and a wellbeing routine that's easy to let slide. Zenith combines planning, study support, and mental health resources into a single, calm home base, so staying consistent doesn't mean carrying five different apps.

Who it's for

Research centered on a core persona: a full-time student balancing academics with everyday life, looking for one simple place to plan, focus, and reset.

SL

Sophie Lee

20 · UNIVERSITY STUDENT · MELBOURNE, AUSTRALIA

Sophie grew up in a supportive family that values education and wellbeing. She moved to the city for university and is adjusting to independent living — always a high achiever, but university life has tested her balance more than ever. 2nd-year Bachelor of Psychology, full-time student.

WANTS
  • A simple way to plan her day and priorities
  • Tools to build and track healthy habits
  • Mindfulness and self-care support
  • Focus features that reduce distractions
PAIN POINTS
  • Feels overwhelmed by assignments and deadlines
  • Forgets tasks or important dates
  • Mental clutter and overthinking affect her focus and sleep
  • Hard to maintain consistency in habits
"I want to feel in control of my day, my mind, and my future."

Mapping the journey

From first hearing about Zenith to forming a sustainable routine, the journey map below tracks Sophie's actions, mindset, and the opportunities each stage surfaced for design.

01

Discover & download

ACTIONS

Hears about Zenith during a stressful week; downloads the app.

THOUGHT

"I need something to help me get my life together — fast."

OPPORTUNITY

Highlight quick relief and make onboarding smooth and inviting.

02

Initial setup

ACTIONS

Sets up core categories that match her routine.

THOUGHT

"Okay, this already feels more organized."

OPPORTUNITY

Smart defaults and templates that ask little of a busy first-time user.

03

Plan & organize

ACTIONS

Uses the calendar to see time blocks and conflicts; filters by color to focus.

THOUGHT

"Now I can actually see what I have to do."

OPPORTUNITY

Show priorities clearly with color and help surface free time around conflicts.

04

Adjust daily plans

ACTIONS

Updates plans as deadlines shift; stays flexible and keeps priorities top of mind.

THOUGHT

"Things are changing, but I can keep up."

OPPORTUNITY

Let users drag-and-drop to reschedule easily, with both a daily focus and a longer view.

05

Manage stress

ACTIONS

Opens the wellbeing space for a quick breathing exercise or journaling.

THOUGHT

"I just need a few minutes to reset."

OPPORTUNITY

Gentle check-ins during high-stress times and one-tap quick-relief features.

06

Weekly review

ACTIONS

Reviews a short summary of the week's habits and mood.

THOUGHT

"I see what worked — and what didn't."

OPPORTUNITY

Insight summaries (e.g. "you were most stressed on Tuesdays") and suggested habits or resources.

End state: feels more organized and in control · finds relief during stressful times · gains insight into habits and needs, and begins forming sustainable routines.

Design process

Before any screens, the core flows started as quick pen-and-paper wireframes — home dashboard, sign in, and sign up — to work out structure before committing to visual design. From there, usability feedback on the early build pointed at two specific friction points. Both "before" screens and the reasoning behind each change are kept here as real design documentation — the corresponding "after" Figma comps were a visibly earlier, less refined design direction than the final build, so rather than show that in-between step, the reasoning below leads straight into the finished product on the live build.

Early wireframes: homepage, sign in, and sign up.

BEFORE — NAVIGATION

Tab targets were easy to mis-tap

Extended the tab length to reduce mis-taps.

BEFORE — CALENDAR

Adding an event broke the flow

Replaced the full-page pop-up with an on-screen edit modal.

Quick-entry journaling with category tags.

Home dashboard with calendar and upcoming schedule.
